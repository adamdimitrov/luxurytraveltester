  • 💯 I even fact-checked him and of course he nailed it!

⏱️ Number of stops/ duration

I was the last pick-up. Besides me, there were two other groups: two French women and a Portuguese family of three. Since I was the last, we headed straight to the first meeting point.

During the briefing, Ismail told me I’d be the first to be dropped off at the end of the tour - great news!

📌 Delays or waiting time

‍‍There were absolutely no delays or waiting times throughout the 7-hour tour, which is almost miraculous!

📌 Distance and travel time (1 hour)

The 90 km drive took about 60 minutes. Thanks to the interesting commentary, the ride flew by. It only started to get a bit uncomfortable in the back row after about 55 minutes right before we arrived at our first stop at the meeting point.

📌 First Desert Stop: Quad / Buggy meeting point (1 hour)

The meeting point for Excursion Point is one of the highest quality I've seen:

  • spotlessly clean restrooms,
  • ‍lush green grass,
  • an air-conditioned indoor area with traditional scarves and clothing for sale,
  • and a fleet of brand-new quads and well-maintained buggies.

📌 Buggy Experience - Worth the Upgrade?

The buggy ride is the highlight of the desert experience! Offering far more power and safety than quad bikes and delivered a massive shot of adrenaline:

  • 1000cc engines vs. 250cc on quads (~4x more power)
  • Huge suspension travel, effortlessly flying over dunes
  • ~45 minutes total (30 min ride, 10 min photo stop, 5 min briefing) vs. 30 min.

This is also reflected in the price as a negative.

The ride depends a lot on your guide and the group (speed, route, etc.). Safety comes first and when I pushed too far, I got a firm warning that nearly ended my ride. (I was a bad, bad boy)

‍Surprise bonus: Because I’m a skilled buggy rider and was patient with the slower group, they rewarded me with an extra 5-minute solo ride with another guide.

It turned into a 15-minute thrill session with higher speed, making the whole experience even truly unforgettable. Best detour of the day!

Pricing?

  • On-site price: 800 AED (€200) for 30 minutes
  • Online deals: As low as 320 AED (~€80) on GetYourGuide (Book in advance to save big!)

We spent about an hour here with the rides + refreshments before heading deeper into the desert at 17:00.

🛂 Sandboarding (10 min)

Right after my unforgettable falcon encounter, I headed straight to the sandboarding spot. For safety reasons, they initially didn’t want me to go standing, but Ismail (my honorary stepdad by this point) made it happen anyway. 💪

Murphy’s Law hit: after nailing every standing ride on previous safaris, this was the time I fell. My shirt didn't get cleaner from this... :D

The guides were incredibly patient and actually took time to explain how to ride sitting down, what no one else had done before. So I gave the sitting style a try and surprise! I had the fastest longest and smoothest ride without fall!

There is always a small competition for the longest ride amongst the group members and until the very end I held the top spot! 🏅 So if you live in a desert arrive prepared! :P

📝 Sandboarding Tips:

  • 📱 Always hand over your phone before sliding.
  • 👖 Don’t keep anything in your pockets that could be damaged by sand.
  • 🏃 Even if you're a pro, the walk back up the dune will guarantee sand in your shoes!
💡 Tip: If you’re tall, try to grab a middle or front row seat for full comfort and a better view.
💡Tip: Book online in advance. For €80 it’s a once-in-a-lifetime experience. At on-site prices, it’s a harder decision.

🏜️ Dune Bashing (20 minutes intense + 10 minutes light ride)

Ismail delivered a brutally fun ride. Before we started he checked if everyone has the seatbelt on then he really floored it. The suspension handled most of the bumps, but with the two heaviest guests in the back, we got a few hits.

  • Vehicle handling: Tires were fully deflated (almost flat), which increases surface area → better traction → smoother, safer turns.  A trick I’d also seen at Ammar Tours, one of their main competitors. Most other operators deflate only partway.

🦅 📸 Falcon Photo in the Open Desert

These birds are legends: calm, beautifully trained, and surprisingly photogenic. But here’s the thing no one tells you: falcons eat… and falcons poop. 😅

That being said my favorite Shirt didn’t survive the encounter. I walked away with a unique souvenir in a lovely white-greenish shade, right across my shoulder. (No worries, I’ve mostly forgiven the bird.) At least I got a great photo and a story to tell.

The photo op usually costs around 20 AED (~5 EUR), and yes, you can pay by credit card. 💳 Just maybe don’t wear your Sunday best. 😄🦅

📸 Photo Stop (≈5 min)

We paused for photos, and our guide proactively offered to take fun group shots. I was too busy dealing with falcon drama, so most of mine are bird-themed.

📌 Inflating area (≈10 min)

After a gentler round of dune bashing, we returned to inflate the tires. There’s a small shop for snacks or drinks. I combined the two and went with a fresh coconut.

As a thoughtful extra, the team cleaned the sand from our shoes.

They did a solid job, but of course, Ismail went above and beyond—making sure even my shirt was in the best shape possible after the falcon “incident.”

These are the little extras you don’t get on every tour.
